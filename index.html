Thesis / Operating Model · Business Model of 2030

When intelligence and software become abundant, code alone ceases to be a lasting advantage. As AI, robotics, and superintelligence mature, traditional software boundaries fade. Every property transaction will become a technology transaction, but lasting value will not sit in a public listing feed. Value concentrates in verified physical evidence and permissioned professional networks.

Business Model of 2030

Verified Reality and Permissioned Distribution

Verified Reality (InspectOS and RealOS)

Developing physical verification, non-scrapable ground-truth data streams, and regulatory compliance standards.

Permissioned Distribution (Speaking Bricks and EstateOS)

Building trust-gated protocols where authorized supply meets represented demand, helping professionals filter out open-market automated noise.

The Market Shift: Information Asymmetry Collapse

Legacy institutions often monetized exclusive access to data. As AI reduces the cost of processing information, that asymmetry dissolves, flooding the open market with synthetic noise. The primary challenge is no longer finding information; the challenge is verifying it.

The Opportunity: Replacing Trust with Proof

When everyone has access to synthetic data, unverified claims lose their value. Advanced AI can process digital files instantly, but it cannot physically inspect a building or legally insure physical reality on its own. In Portuguese property, I am deploying this verification layer through Skaler. With InspectOS, transactions are supported by documented, physical evidence, generating structured data backed by accountable human professionals.

The Moat: The Vertical Data Flywheel

Compounds with every transaction

Rather than building generic horizontal tools, I focus deeply on specific industry verticals to create a compounding flywheel.

InspectOS conducts on-site technical inspections, capturing structured physical evidence and defect data.

As professionals use these tools to verify assets and coordinate transactions, they generate structured, vertical-specific proprietary data within permitted boundaries. This feedback loop continually improves operations and creates a durable advantage over generalist software competitors.

Separation of Economics

To maintain operational clarity and protect partner interests, every venture separates asset ownership from operating company risk:

Owner Economics

Asset owners retain title to their underlying assets and establish clear access arrangements, such as a license, lease, or service agreement, alongside agreed equity participation. Liability, insurance, and closure obligations are defined upfront before the company uses the asset.

Operating Economics

The new operating venture manages the technology stack, customer acquisition, daily delivery, and operating costs. Contributions, ownership, vesting, milestones, and decision rights are agreed upon before committing substantial resources.

The Five Moats of the Post-Software Era

When software development becomes faster and cheaper, what makes a new company in a traditional industry durable? The answer lies in the friction of the real world and owned channels.

I.
Avoiding the Commodity Software Trap
The traditional software model is facing rapid deflation. When code generation and standard product features are easily automated, purely digital advantages can erode quickly. If a business lives entirely on a screen without real-world integration, customer access, or proprietary inputs, its margins will face constant pressure.
II.
Ground-Truth Data
A durable information advantage requires permitted access to data that cannot be easily scraped or synthesized. Ground-truth data originates at the physical edge: calibrated measurements from field instruments, verified operational logs, reconciled legal documents, and localized physical assessments. Anchoring software workflows to these real-world inputs allows service quality to improve with repeated delivery.
III.
Regulatory Compliance as a Structural Shield
An automated agent can draft a property summary in seconds, but it cannot hold a professional license or accept legal responsibility. In European markets, professional liability frameworks, technical accreditations, the EU AI Act, and data protection rules represent significant operational responsibilities. Rather than avoiding regulated complexity, I design companies to meet these standards directly. By anchoring technology to qualified human professionals and strict independence standards, regulatory compliance becomes a durable barrier to entry.
IV.
Process Power and Workflow Depth
Professional buyers and enterprises do not purchase raw AI output; they purchase reliable outcomes, risk reduction, and clear accountability. Embedding verified data and clear status protocols directly into the daily workflows of lawyers, representatives, and asset managers creates lasting process power and natural switching costs.
V.
Owned and Permissioned Distribution
When software is abundant, trusted access to decision-makers becomes the primary commercial bottleneck. Public search and open portals are increasingly crowded with automated noise. Distribution must be designed alongside the product from day one. Building permissioned professional networks and direct industry relationships gives operating companies a clear, testable route to market without relying exclusively on rented advertising channels.
